# Six Selected Apollo Moonquake Datasets for Musification

---

## No. 1 — `xa.s11.00.mhz.1969.202.0.mseed`

**Date:** 21 July 1969 · **Julian day:** 202 · **Station:** Apollo 11 (S11)

Julian day 202 of 1969 is 21 July 1969. That is the exact day the Apollo 11 Passive Seismic Experiment was turned on during the EVA (~04:39 UTC) and recorded the LM ascent/liftoff at 17:54 UTC, plus the astronaut footfalls, hammer strokes, and other surface activity. The PDS archive uses this standard daily filename convention, so this file is the continuous vertical mid-period record for that historic day.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s11/1969/202/

## No. 2 — `xa.s11.00.mhz.1969.207.0.mseed`

**Date:** 26 July 1969 · **Julian day:** 207 · **Station:** Apollo 11 (S11)

**Description:** Mid-first-lunar-day. LM thermal/venting noise has largely subsided; classic L-events (long-duration, emergent, ringing) and possible thermal moonquakes are clearer. Average ~4 L-type events per day across the whole period.

**Sonic character:** Cleaner natural lunar signals, good signal-to-noise for pure moonquake textures.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s11/1969/207/xa.s11.00.mhz.1969.207.0.mseed

## No. 3 — `xa.s11.00.mhz.1969.214.0.mseed`

**Date:** 2 August 1969 · **Julian day:** 214 (215 was the alternative) · **Station:** Apollo 11 (S11)

**Description:** Near the end of the first lunar day, approaching lunar noon. Higher thermal cracking and longer ringing events; different spectral character from the early days.

**Sonic character:** More sustained, slowly building thermal-type energy.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s11/1969/214/xa.s11.00.mhz.1969.214.0.mseed

## No. 4 — `xa.s12.00.mhz.1969.324.0.mseed`

**Date:** 20 November 1969 · **Julian day:** 324 · **Station:** Apollo 12 (S12)

**Description:** Historic controlled impact day. Contains the deliberate Apollo 12 LM Ascent Stage impact at **22:17:17.7 UTC** (~76 km from the seismometer). First large, precisely timed artificial seismic source recorded on the Moon.

**Sonic character:** Sharp onset followed by slow 7-minute build and ~50–55 minute ringing decay. Clean, dramatic, and highly narrative.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s12/1969/324/xa.s12.00.mhz.1969.324.0.mseed

---

## No. 5 — `xa.s12.00.mhz.1973.072.0.mseed`

**Date:** 13 March 1973 · **Julian day:** 072 · **Station:** Apollo 12 (S12)

**Description:** Day of **YN-SMQ-9**, a very large natural shallow moonquake (minimum body-wave magnitude >6.5). One of the strongest natural events in the entire Apollo catalog.

**Sonic character:** Powerful, long-duration emergent energy with sustained high-amplitude ringing. Excellent pure moonquake texture.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s12/1973/072/xa.s12.00.mhz.1973.072.0.mseed

---

## No. 6 — `xa.s12.00.mhz.1976.066.0.mseed`

**Date:** 6 March 1976 · **Julian day:** 066 · **Station:** Apollo 12 (S12)

**Description:** Day of **YN-SMQ-26**, a very large natural shallow moonquake (minimum body-wave magnitude >6.1). High-energy event well-recorded later in the Apollo 12 mission.

**Sonic character:** Powerful, long-duration emergent energy with sustained high-amplitude ringing and clear high-frequency content. Excellent pure moonquake texture.

**REF:** https://pds-geosciences.wustl.edu/lunar/urn-nasa-pds-apollo_pse/data/xa/continuous_waveform/s12/1976/066/xa.s12.00.mhz.1976.066.0.mseed

## Summary

These six continuous MHZ (vertical mid-period) MiniSEED files were selected for moonquake musification. The set balances historical significance, sonic variety, and data quality while following a "less is more" approach.

**Apollo 11 (S11)** provides the first seismic recordings from the Moon, including the day the instrument was activated and the LM ascent, plus two later days with cleaner natural signals and different thermal character.

**Apollo 12 (S12)** contributes the longest-running and cleanest station in the network, featuring the famous controlled LM ascent stage impact plus two of the largest natural shallow moonquakes recorded by the Apollo seismic experiment.
